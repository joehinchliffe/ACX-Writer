### Reading Level Guidelines

Use these definitions to identify **one** reading level that best matches the manuscript’s complexity. If no clear match emerges, mark as “N/A.”

1. **Basic Reading Level (6-7)**  
   - Intended for younger readers or those just starting to read independently.  
   - Simple language, short sentences, limited dense information, restricted vocabulary.  
   - Examples: Children’s books, basic manuals.  
   - Common Terms: Beginner, Early Learner, Children’s Non-Fiction.  
   - Typical Titles: “Sport Stories for Kids”

2. **Intermediate Level (8-10, most common)**  
   - Suited for readers with a good understanding of language and text structure but not yet experts.  
   - More detailed coverage, richer vocabulary, introduces more complex concepts simply.  
   - Examples: Basic psychology or philosophy, practical self-improvement guides.  
   - Common Terms: Intermediate, Young Adult (YA), Guidebooks, Practical Non-Fiction.  
   - Typical Titles: “How to Stop Procrastination”

3. **Advanced Level (11-12)**  
   - For experienced readers comfortable with complex or technical concepts and rich vocabulary.  
   - Covers topics in-depth, using sophisticated language and academic references.  
   - Examples: Academic books, professional manuals, research-based writing.  
   - Common Terms: Advanced, Scholarly, Academic, Professional.  
   - Typical Titles: “The Selfish Gene: Understanding Evolutionary Biology”

### Writing Style Guidelines

Select **one or more** styles from the list below based on the content and tone suggested by the DRP and Book Outline. If no clear style is indicated, mark as “N/A.”

1. **Formal**  
   - Professional, precise; avoids contractions, slang, or colloquialisms.  
   - Common Niches: Business, Finance, Science, Technology.  

2. **Informal**  
   - Relaxed, conversational; may use colloquial expressions and personal touches.  
   - Common Niches: Personal Development, Teens/Young Adults, Creative pursuits.  

3. **Serious**  
   - Straightforward, no-nonsense; avoids humor or fluff.  
   - Common Niches: Psychology, Health, Medicine.  

4. **Humorous**  
   - Uses wit, jokes, or satire to engage; must be audience-appropriate.  
   - Common Niches: Entertainment, comedic takes on daily life, parenting.  

5. **Authoritative**  
   - Clear, confident, and directive; conveys expertise and credibility.  
   - Common Niches: Investing, Money Management, Technology, Computers.  

6. **Supportive/Empathetic**  
   - Reflects emotions and encourages; fosters a deeper connection with readers.  
   - Common Niches: Self-Help, Motivational, Health/Wellness, Faith-Based.  

7. **Open/Vulnerable**  
   - Shares personal experiences and emotions; introspective.  
   - Common Niches: Memoirs, Life Coaching, personal stories.  

8. **Friendly Casual**  
   - Feels like chatting with a friend; warm and engaging.  
   - Common Niches: Travel Guides, Hobby Books, Children’s Educational Content.  

9. **Personal**  
   - Similar to friendly casual but with more individual reflections and anecdotes.  
   - Common Niches: Relationships, Dating Advice, Motivational, Addiction Recovery.  

10. **Narrative**  
   - Storytelling-based, using characters, settings, anecdotes for immersion.  
   - Common Niches: Historical topics, Kids’/YA Fiction, Cultural/Social issues, Personal Development.
