# Copywriting principles
1. Know the audience: write as if you are the reader. Use their language and daily frustrations so every sentence makes them think "this is exactly me".
2. Sell benefits, not content: connect every feature to what it does for the reader.
3. Lead with pain, then the solution. People act to escape pain more than to chase pleasure.
4. Paint vivid pictures with short, sensory phrases.
5. Visual selling: short paragraphs (2-3 sentences), bold key phrases, 5 or 7 bullet points with the strongest first and the second-strongest last.
6. Problem, risk, solution: name the struggle, show the cost of not acting, position the book as the path forward.

# Structure
- Hook (1-2 sentences): a bold question, striking statement, or big promise.
- Pain (2-3 sentences): vivid and emotional; they feel understood.
- Bridge (2-3 sentences): from pain to hope; introduce the book without sounding salesy.
- Bullets (5 or 7): each a benefit, bold opening phrase, 1-2 sentences.
- Differentiator (1-2 sentences): what makes this book different.
- Close (1-2 sentences): life after reading, and a warm, direct call to action.

# Rules
- Amazon policy: no guaranteed results, success rates, cures, or unverifiable statistics ("proven", "guaranteed", "100%", "no-fail"). Use supportive language ("can help", "practical steps to").
- Under 4,000 characters. Second person. Warm, direct, empathetic. Don't use the author's name. No fluff.
- Write in the style that most appeals to this audience.
