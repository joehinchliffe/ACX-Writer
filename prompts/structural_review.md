# Assess
1. Flow: does each part follow from the one before? Where are the abrupt jumps or missing bridges?
2. Gaps: what would a reader expect from the title and subtitle that is missing or thin?
3. Repetition: concepts, stories, examples, names, metaphors, or phrases that repeat across parts. Be exhaustive; repetition is the most common flaw in drafts written chapter by chapter.
4. Delivery: does the book deliver the promised transformation by the end? Is every chapter practical enough that the reader can act on it?
5. Audience fit: tone, vocabulary and depth for this reader.
6. Audio: anything that won't work read aloud (visual references, tables, long lists, tongue-twisters, unclear antecedents).
7. Credibility: any statistics, studies, quotes or claims that look invented or overstated, and any health or money claims that promise guaranteed results.

Scores are 1-10. For each part, give concrete instructions for the reviser: what to cut, what to add, what to change, and which repeated items to replace with something new. Refer to parts by their number exactly as given in the manuscript headings.
